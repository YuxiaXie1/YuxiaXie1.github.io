My work follows a simple question: how can we make complex data useful for understanding the systems that shape human health?

YUXIA XIE · DUKE UNIVERSITY
THE SHORT VERSION

I am a postdoctoral associate in Biostatistics and Bioinformatics at Duke University, a Duke Cancer Institute trainee, and an investigator with the Center for Human Systems Immunology. I am also an NIH/NCI K99–R00 Pathway to Independence Awardee, building an independent research program in computational biology and AI for oncology.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Tongji University in 2023. My doctoral research focused on machine learning for large-scale distributed systems: finding anomalies, predicting failures, and making complex systems easier to diagnose. That training still shapes how I think about biomedical data—models need to be robust, interpretable, and grounded in the structure of the problem.

At Duke, I bring those ideas to single-cell and spatial biology. I develop graph-based and cross-modal methods to map cellular ecosystems, infer pathway activity, and connect tissue architecture with molecular measurements. My long-term aim is to build tools that help explain tumor biology and reveal biomarkers relevant to treatment response.
