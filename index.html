About

A research profile designed to be clear, memorable, and useful.

My work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, data analysis, and computational biology. I enjoy translating difficult biological questions into rigorous computational problems, then building practical models that help reveal meaningful patterns in complex data.

Before joining Duke University, I earned my PhD in Computer Science at Tongji University, where I studied anomaly detection, prediction, and failure diagnosis for large-scale computing systems. Today, I bring that systems mindset into biomedical research, especially in multi-omics and imaging applications.
